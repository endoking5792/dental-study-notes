## EXAM MODE

Microleakage is the clinically undetectable passage of bacteria, fluids and ions along the cavity–restoration interface (Sikri, 2020, p.435). Its clinical implications are:

1. **Postoperative sensitivity.** Leakage links oral fluid to the pulp and moves fluid in the dentinal tubules. Worse in proximal and cervical cavities and with resin (Sikri, 2020, p.435).
2. **Recurrent caries.** Bacteria pass gaps of about 10 µm, which then supply nutrients for growth (Sikri, 2020, p.435).
3. **Pulpal pathology.** Bacteria and their products reach the pulp through dentinal tubules (Sikri, 2020, p.435–436).
4. **Marginal discoloration** that is hard to remove and often needs replacement (Sikri, 2020, p.436).
5. **Breakdown of luting cements** (Sikri, 2020, p.436).
6. Kidd (1976, review) also lists tooth discoloration under amalgams, hypersensitivity of restored teeth, pulpal damage and hastened breakdown of some filling materials (PDF p.1) [LIT, full text].
