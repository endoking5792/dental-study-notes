## STUDY MODE (lean, 10 marks)
Microleakage is the clinically undetectable passage of bacteria, fluids, molecules or ions along the cavity–restoration interface (Sikri, 2020, p.435). A material is evaluated on the properties that open the gap and on how it behaves in practice:

- **CTE mismatch** with tooth (11.4 ×10⁻⁶/°C): amalgam 25.0 and composite 20–25 mismatch most; glass-ionomer 11.0 and gold 14.0 least (Sikri, 2020, p.436, Table 22.1).
- **Polymerization shrinkage**: acrylic heat-cure about 7%; conventional composite 1.5–2.0%; microfilled 1.3–1.5% (Sikri, 2020, p.436, Table 22.2).
- **Adhesion**: wetting, surface energy, water, smear layer (Sikri, 2020, p.436–437).
- **Material-specific behaviour**: amalgam leaves a 10–15 µm gap, then self-seals with corrosion products (Sikri, 2020, p.438). GIC bonds chemically and releases fluoride but needs 24 hours of moisture control (Sikri, 2020, p.439). Composite leaks mainly at the dentin bond (Sikri, 2020, p.440). Gold adapts by burnishing (Sikri, 2020, p.443). Cast restorations leave 10–160 µm gaps (Sikri, 2020, p.444).

## EXAM MODE

**Introduction.** Microleakage is the passage of bacteria, fluids and ions along the cavity–restoration gap (Sikri, 2020, p.435). No restorative material bonds perfectly to tooth structure, so every material is judged on how far it keeps the gap closed.

**1. Properties evaluated**
- *Thermal expansion.* A large mismatch with tooth (11.4 ×10⁻⁶/°C) opens and closes the gap with temperature. Amalgam (25) and composite (20–25) are the worst; glass-ionomer (11) and gold (14) are the best (Sikri, 2020, p.436).
- *Polymerization shrinkage.* Resins shrink as monomer forms polymer. Shrinkage stress breaks the adhesive bond; microfilled resins shrink least (Sikri, 2020, p.436).
- *Adhesion.* Depends on wetting, surface energy, water, smear layer and surface roughness (Sikri, 2020, p.436–437).

**2. Material-by-material**
- *Amalgam:* 10–15 µm gap when fresh; corrosion products seal it with time; high-copper alloys leak for longer (Sikri, 2020, p.438).
- *Glass-ionomer:* chemical adhesion and fluoride release; sensitive to moisture for 24 hours (Sikri, 2020, p.439).
- *Composite:* dentin bond fails first under shrinkage; enamel bond gives near-nil leakage (Sikri, 2020, p.440).
- *Gold:* burnishes into the margin; cohesive foil seals better than mat or powdered gold (Sikri, 2020, p.443).
- *Cast:* 10–160 µm gaps; luting cement needed (Sikri, 2020, p.444).

**3. Interpretation.** Amalgam and gold show the most stable seal over time; composite depends most on the bonding protocol; glass-ionomer depends on moisture control. Evaluation must look at both the material properties and the operator. Methods differ widely between studies (Taylor and Lynch, 1992, full text), and in-vitro bond and gap data correlate only weakly with clinical outcomes (Peutzfeldt et al, 2024, full text).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Microleakage = clinically undetectable passage along the interface (Sikri, 2020, p.435).
- Three properties: CTE, shrinkage, adhesion (Sikri, 2020, p.436).
- Amalgam: self-seals with corrosion products (Sikri, 2020, p.438).
- GIC: moisture control for 24 hours (Sikri, 2020, p.439).
- Composite: dentin bond fails first (Sikri, 2020, p.440).

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
1. Listing materials without the property that causes the leakage.
2. Calling amalgam "non-leaking" from day one; a fresh amalgam leaves a gap.
3. Forgetting the operator factor (packing, isolation, finishing).
