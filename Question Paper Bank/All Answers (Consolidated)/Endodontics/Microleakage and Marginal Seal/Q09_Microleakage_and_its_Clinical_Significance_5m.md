## EXAM MODE

**Definition.** Microleakage is the clinically undetectable passage of bacteria, fluids, molecules or ions along the interface between a restoration or root filling and the tooth (Sikri, 2020, p.435).

**Clinical significance in endodontics.**
1. Coronal leakage is proposed to contribute to treatment failure, based on in-vitro leakage studies (Berman et al, 2021, PDF p.1068).
2. Obturation reduces coronal leakage and bacterial contamination, seals the apex from periapical fluids, and entombs the remaining irritants (Berman et al, 2021, PDF p.1068).
3. Coronal microleakage can occur quickly through a canal that looks well obturated, risking periapical infection, so the coronal seal is now seen as important as the apical one (Berman et al, 2021, PDF p.1152).

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Microleakage = undetectable passage along the interface (Sikri, 2020, p.435).
- Coronal leakage can cause treatment failure (Berman et al, 2021, PDF p.1068).
- Obturation entombs irritants and reduces coronal leakage (Berman et al, 2021, PDF p.1068).
