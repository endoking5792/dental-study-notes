## EXAM MODE

**Principle.** A dye leakage study places a coloured tracer at a restoration or root-filling interface, then measures how far the dye travels. Dyes used include methylene blue, India ink, crystal violet, fluorescein and rhodamine B (Sikri, 2020, p.445).

**Procedure.** The extracted tooth is immersed in dye for a set time, washed, sectioned and examined under a microscope. Penetration is scored by depth (Sikri, 2020, p.445).

**Limitations.** Results depend on dye particle size and concentration; dyes can bind to carious dentin; trapped air is hard to remove in small spaces; some dyes are not colour-stable; and scoring is subjective (Sikri, 2020, p.445).

**Endodontic findings from dye studies.**
- Cold lateral condensation showed more volumetric leakage than other obturation techniques in in-vitro studies, and dye studies found greater apical leakage with lateral condensation (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019, PDF p.627).
- Dye and bacterial leakage studies showed MTA leaking significantly less than amalgam and Super EBA as a root-end filling (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019, PDF p.818).
- Gutta-percha is described as a poor sealant according to radioisotope and dye studies (Weine, 2004, Ch.7; page not verified).
- All obturation materials and techniques leak to some degree (Berman et al, 2021, PDF p.1069).
- Dye leakage after coronal exposure (Swanson and Madison, 1987, Part I): gutta-percha and sealer-filled anterior teeth exposed to artificial saliva for 3 to 56 days leaked 79 to 85% of the root length. Unexposed controls did not leak, and the time periods did not differ significantly (PDF p.1) [LIT, full text].
- Sealer dye data (Madison et al, 1987, Part II): mean dye penetration was 33% (Sealapex), 49% (Roth's sealer) and 80% (AH26) of root length. AH26 leaked significantly more (p < 0.02) (PDF pp.1–2) [LIT, full text].
- In vivo (Madison and Wilcox, 1988, Part III, monkeys): dye penetrated the canals in all groups, with no significant difference among the sealers. This contrasts with the in-vitro ranking of sealers (PDF p.1) [LIT, full text].

**Interpretation.** Dye studies rank materials and techniques by penetration. Taylor and Lynch (1992, full text) list the dyes used across studies and report wide methodological variation (PDF p.5). They show that a leakage path exists, but the depth scored depends on the dye and the protocol, so results are compared only within one protocol.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- Dyes: methylene blue, India ink, crystal violet, fluorescein, rhodamine B (Sikri, 2020, p.445).
- Method: immerse, section, score depth (Sikri, 2020, p.445).
- Dye errors: particle size, binding, trapped air, colour fading, subjective scoring (Sikri, 2020, p.445).
- MTA leaks less than amalgam and Super EBA in dye and bacterial studies (Rotstein and Ingle, 2019, PDF p.818).

None required at 10 marks.

## COMMON EXAM MISTAKES
1. Treating the dye depth as a precise measure, without the protocol.
2. Forgetting trapped air and dye-binding errors.
3. Giving dye results without the comparison (MTA vs amalgam, lateral vs other techniques).
