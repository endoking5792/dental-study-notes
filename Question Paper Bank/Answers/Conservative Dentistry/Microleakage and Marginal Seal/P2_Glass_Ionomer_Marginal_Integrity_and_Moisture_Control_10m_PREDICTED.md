## EXAM MODE

**Introduction.** Glass-ionomer cement bonds chemically to tooth structure through carboxyl groups and calcium ions, its CTE is close to that of tooth, and it releases fluoride. Its marginal integrity depends on moisture control during and after setting (Sikri, 2020, p.439).

**1. Why it can leak.** The cement is hydrophilic and technique-sensitive. Early on, matrix-forming ions leach out if the cement is exposed to water, which can interfere with the tooth–restoration interface. Solubility continues for about 24 hours after insertion. Excess dehydration causes crazing; if the crazing reaches the margin, leakage is considerable (Sikri, 2020, p.439).

**2. Resin-modified GIC.** Light-cured resin-modified cements set quickly, which reduces moisture contamination, but they have sometimes leaked more than chemically cured cements. Their rapid light-induced contraction is not relieved by slow setting, and they absorb less water and carboxylic acid, which reduces wetting (Sikri, 2020, p.440).

**3. Measures to reduce leakage.**
- Place the cement on clean dentin, with a correct powder:liquid ratio, since a low powder ratio increases solubility (Sikri, 2020, p.440).
- Finish with rotary instruments, since hand instruments can tear margins and cause marginal ditching (Sikri, 2020, p.440).
- Protect the surface with petroleum jelly during finishing, and then seal with two coats of varnish or an unfilled resin. Varnish adheres well to enamel; unfilled resin resists water better (Sikri, 2020, p.440).
- Condition the dentin surface with tannic, polyacrylic or citric acid before placement (Sikri, 2020, p.440).
- Use a light-cured base under a restoration, because it adheres to dentin immediately and resists stress better than chemically cured bases (Sikri, 2020, p.443).

**Interpretation.** Glass-ionomer seals well when moisture is controlled and the surface is protected. Its failure is more often a handling problem than a material weakness.

## HIGH-YIELD POINTS
- GIC bonds chemically; CTE close to tooth; fluoride release (Sikri, 2020, p.439).
- Solubility continues for 24 hours after insertion (Sikri, 2020, p.439).
- Desiccation crazing that reaches the margin leaks (Sikri, 2020, p.439).
- Rotary finishing; varnish or unfilled resin seal (Sikri, 2020, p.440).
- RMGI can leak more than chemically cured GIC (Sikri, 2020, p.440).
