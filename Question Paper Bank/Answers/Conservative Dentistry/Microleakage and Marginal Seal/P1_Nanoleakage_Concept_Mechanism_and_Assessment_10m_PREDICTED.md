## EXAM MODE

**Definition.** Nanoleakage is leakage around collagen fibrils within the hybrid layer that resin has not fully infiltrated. It can sit at the base of the hybrid layer or along its width, depending on the bonding system (Sikri, 2020, p.448).

**Mechanism.** Bonding systems that etch deeper into dentin tend to show more nanoleakage. This is one possible reason self-etching systems leak less than systems that use a separate acid step (Sikri, 2020, p.448). Poorly infiltrated hybrid layers contain voids that hold water and oral fluid. Water accelerates degradation of the bond (Sikri, 2020, p.448).

**Clinical significance.** Incomplete infiltration of demineralized dentin leaves pores. The modulus of the poorly infiltrated layer falls between adhesive and unfilled collagen, and contact with water lowers it further. Slow hydrolysis means the bond does not keep its integrity for long, so dentin bonding agents may reduce early leakage, but their long-term performance under stress and continued fluid exposure is questionable (Sikri, 2020, p.448).

**Assessment.**
- Tracers: silver nitrate (50% aqueous solution, commonly used), silver methenamine and ammoniacal silver nitrate. The hybrid layer is porous and takes up silver even without a marginal gap (Sikri, 2020, p.448).
- Microscopy: SEM for the hybrid layer at 200× to 1000×, with artefacts from drying and embedding; TEM for detail below 1 nm; FESEM with better resolution; CLSEM with rhodamine B, without drying (Sikri, 2020, p.449).

**Interpretation.** Nanoleakage is a hybrid-layer property, not a marginal gap. Its clinical meaning is long-term bond durability, which the laboratory tracer methods do not measure directly.
