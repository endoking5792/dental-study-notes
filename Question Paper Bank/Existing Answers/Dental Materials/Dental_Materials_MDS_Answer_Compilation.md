## 1. Tooth-coloured restorative materials

Mark allocation: 10 marks.

### Study Mode

#### Definition

Tooth-coloured restorative materials are restorative materials selected to approximate the colour and appearance of natural tooth structure while restoring lost tooth tissue and function. [Certain]

#### Classification

Direct materials: resin-based composites, conventional glass ionomer cements, resin-modified glass ionomer cements, compomers and giomer-type materials. [Certain]

Indirect materials: laboratory resin composites and ceramic systems such as feldspathic ceramics, leucite-reinforced ceramics, lithium disilicate and zirconia. [Certain]

#### Important properties

| Property | Clinical significance |
|---|---|
| Esthetics | Shade matching, translucency, fluorescence and surface gloss influence the final appearance. [Certain] |
| Bonding | Adhesive bonding can permit conservative preparation and improves retention of many tooth-coloured restorations. [Certain] |
| Polymerization shrinkage | Relevant mainly to resin-based materials because shrinkage can contribute to interfacial stress and marginal defects. [Certain] |
| Water sorption | Can affect dimensional stability, mechanical behaviour and colour stability of resin-based materials. [Certain] |
| Thermal behaviour | Mismatch between tooth and restorative material can contribute to thermal stresses. [Certain] |
| Radiopacity | Adequate radiopacity assists detection of recurrent caries and assessment of restoration margins. [Certain] |
| Fluoride/ion release | Important particularly for glass ionomer based materials and some ion-releasing restorative systems. [Certain] |

#### Major direct materials

Resin composite: high esthetic potential, adhesive placement and broad indications, but polymerization shrinkage, technique sensitivity and wear remain important considerations. [Certain]

Conventional GIC: chemically bonds to tooth structure, releases fluoride and has a coefficient of thermal expansion relatively compatible with tooth structure, but is weaker and more moisture-sensitive than resin composite. [Certain]

RMGIC: combines an acid-base reaction with resin polymerization and generally has improved early strength and handling compared with conventional GIC. [Certain]

Compomer: polyacid-modified resin composite with characteristics intermediate between composite and GIC, although it does not behave as a conventional GIC. [Certain]

Giomer and other ion-releasing materials: incorporate ion-releasing components within resin-based systems, but evidence for superiority over conventional composite remains limited for several clinical outcomes. [Likely]

#### Recent advances

Bulk-fill resin composites have been developed to permit thicker increments and simplify posterior restoration, with contemporary formulations showing acceptable polymerization and mechanical performance, although shrinkage and depth of cure remain formulation-dependent. [Likely]

Bioactive or ion-releasing resin materials have been introduced with the aim of providing remineralization-related or caries-inhibitory effects, but a 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis found no significant advantage over conventional composite for secondary caries or retention loss across the included randomized trials. [Certain]

Current evidence therefore supports treating 'bioactive' as a material characteristic rather than automatically assuming superior clinical performance. [Certain]

### Exam Mode

Definition: Tooth-coloured restorative materials reproduce the appearance of tooth structure while restoring form and function. [Certain]

Classification: direct and indirect materials. [Certain]

Direct: composite resin, GIC, RMGIC, compomer and giomer-type materials. [Certain]

Indirect: laboratory composite and ceramic systems including feldspathic, leucite, lithium disilicate and zirconia. [Certain]

Advantages: esthetics, conservative adhesive preparations, acceptable function and versatility. [Certain]

Limitations: polymerization shrinkage, technique sensitivity, wear, moisture sensitivity and possible colour change depending on material. [Certain]

GIC/RMGIC provide fluoride release and chemical adhesion, but have lower mechanical performance than many resin composites. [Certain]

Ceramics provide high esthetic quality and mechanical performance, but usually require laboratory or CAD/CAM procedures. [Certain]

Advances include bulk-fill composites and ion-releasing or bioactive resin systems. [Certain]

Critical point: newer ion-releasing claims should not be equated with proven clinical superiority. [Certain]

## 2. Discuss critically the cavity liners and bases

Mark allocation: 50 marks.

### Study Mode

#### Introduction

Cavity liners and bases are materials placed between the restorative material and the remaining tooth structure for selected biologic, mechanical, thermal or chemical purposes. [Certain] Modern operative dentistry has shifted from routine bulk placement toward selective use based on remaining dentin thickness, pulpal status, restorative material and the quality of the final coronal seal. [Likely]

#### Definitions

Liner: a relatively thin layer of material placed on selected areas of the cavity, traditionally to provide a specific biologic or protective effect. [Certain]

Base: a thicker layer of material placed to replace missing dentin, provide support or provide thermal, chemical or mechanical protection. [Certain]

#### Objectives

Protection of the pulp from chemical, thermal and mechanical irritation. [Certain]

Reduction of postoperative sensitivity where indicated. [Certain]

Provision of a suitable substrate beneath the definitive restorative material. [Certain]

Biologic stimulation of hard-tissue repair in selected pulp exposures or deep lesions. [Certain]

Replacement of missing dentin when a cavity requires bulk replacement or support. [Certain]

#### Classification

| Material | Typical role | Important limitations |
|---|---|---|
| Calcium hydroxide | Liner in very deep areas and direct/indirect pulp protection. [Certain] | Low strength and relatively poor mechanical properties; should be used selectively. [Certain] |
| Zinc oxide-eugenol | Sedative temporary material, liner/base in selected situations and temporary cementation. [Certain] | Eugenol can interfere with resin polymerization. [Certain] |
| Zinc phosphate | Traditional base/luting cement. [Certain] | No chemical adhesion to tooth and initially acidic. [Certain] |
| Polycarboxylate | Luting and selected base applications. [Certain] | Lower strength and less versatile than modern adhesive alternatives. [Likely] |
| Conventional GIC | Base/liner and restorative use in selected situations. [Certain] | Moisture sensitivity during early setting and lower fracture resistance than resin-based materials. [Certain] |
| RMGIC | Base/liner and dentin replacement in selected cavities. [Certain] | Contains resin and therefore retains polymerization-related considerations. [Certain] |

#### Calcium hydroxide

Calcium hydroxide is strongly alkaline and has antibacterial activity. In direct pulp protection, its high pH can produce superficial tissue necrosis followed by an inflammatory response and subsequent hard-tissue barrier formation. [Certain] Conventional self-cure calcium hydroxide liners have low tensile and compressive strength and a low modulus, so they should be restricted to small, deep areas rather than used as a bulk base. [Certain]

#### Zinc oxide-eugenol

Zinc oxide-eugenol materials have long been used because of their sealing and sedative characteristics and their usefulness in temporary restorations and cementation. [Certain] Eugenol can inhibit polymerization of methacrylate resin systems, making noneugenol alternatives preferable when an adhesive resin procedure is planned. [Certain]

#### Glass ionomer and resin-modified glass ionomer

Glass ionomer materials chemically interact with tooth structure and release fluoride. [Certain] Resin-modified glass ionomers incorporate resin components and generally offer improved early strength and handling compared with conventional GIC. [Certain] Contemporary operative dentistry commonly favors RMGIC when a substantial base or dentin-replacement layer is actually required, while calcium hydroxide may be restricted to the deepest area. [Likely]

#### Critical appraisal: is routine lining necessary?

The weakest traditional assumption is that every deep cavity needs a liner or base. [Certain] A 2025 systematic review of liners versus no liner in deep caries of vital permanent teeth included 12 studies and found no significant differences in several outcomes including failure, postoperative hypersensitivity and secondary caries, although the certainty of evidence was low or very low. [Certain] This supports a selective rather than routine approach to cavity lining. [Likely]

#### Depth-based clinical approach

Assess pulpal diagnosis and restorability before selecting a liner or base. [Certain]

Preserve sound dentin whenever possible rather than removing tooth structure solely to create space for a base. [Certain]

In a very deep area close to the pulp, calcium hydroxide may be used selectively where its biologic indication is appropriate. [Certain]

A protective RMGIC layer can be used when a dentin-replacement or base function is genuinely required. [Likely]

Avoid placing weak calcium hydroxide as a thick bulk base. [Certain]

Avoid eugenol-containing materials beneath definitive resin procedures when residual eugenol could interfere with polymerization. [Certain]

Prioritize an effective adhesive interface and a durable coronal seal. [Certain]

#### Critical comparison

| Approach | Rationale | Critical interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Routine liner/base in all deep cavities | Traditional pulpal protection concept. [Certain] | Not strongly supported by contemporary evidence for routine liner use. [Certain] |
| Selective calcium hydroxide | Biologic use in very deep areas. [Certain] | Useful in selected indications but mechanically weak. [Certain] |
| RMGIC as dentin replacement/base | Better mechanical handling and fluoride release than conventional GIC. [Certain] | Useful when a true base/dentin replacement layer is needed. [Likely] |
| No liner when not indicated | Preserves dentin and avoids unnecessary interfaces. [Likely] | Supported by recent low-certainty evidence in deep caries. [Certain] |

### Exam Mode

State indications and objectives. [Certain]

Classify: Ca(OH)2, ZOE, zinc phosphate, polycarboxylate, GIC and RMGIC. [Certain]

Describe Ca(OH)2: high pH, antibacterial effect, superficial necrosis and hard-tissue barrier formation. [Certain]

State the major limitation of Ca(OH)2: low mechanical strength. [Certain]

Describe ZOE and its eugenol-resin incompatibility. [Certain]

Describe GIC/RMGIC: adhesion, fluoride release and clinical uses. [Certain]

Conclude: preserve dentin, use the least material necessary, and prioritize the definitive restoration and coronal seal. [Likely]

## 3. Cavity liners

Mark allocation: 10 marks.

### Study Mode

A cavity liner is a thin layer placed on selected areas of a prepared cavity to provide a specific biologic or protective effect. [Certain] Traditional liners include calcium hydroxide and selected zinc oxide-based materials, while modern practice may use resin-modified glass ionomer or calcium-silicate materials in specific indications. [Certain]

#### Functions

Pulp protection. [Certain]

Antibacterial or high-pH effects where applicable. [Certain]

Promotion of hard-tissue response in selected pulp-protection procedures. [Certain]

Reduction of sensitivity in selected clinical circumstances. [Likely]

Provision of a thin protective interface beneath the definitive restoration. [Certain]

#### Important liners

| Liner | Key point |
|---|---|
| Calcium hydroxide | High pH, antibacterial action and hard-tissue bridge formation, but weak mechanically. [Certain] |
| RMGIC | Adhesive, fluoride-releasing and stronger than conventional CaOH2 liners. [Certain] |
| Calcium-silicate materials | Bioactive/hydraulic materials increasingly used for vital pulp therapy and pulp protection. [Certain] |

#### Critical point

Routine placement of a liner under every deep restoration is difficult to justify. [Certain] The 2025 systematic review of deep caries found no significant advantage for routine cavity lining across several clinical outcomes, although evidence certainty was low or very low. [Certain]

### Exam Mode

Functions: pulp protection, antibacterial effect, biologic stimulation and selected sensitivity control. [Certain]

Types: calcium hydroxide, RMGIC and selected calcium-silicate materials. [Certain]

CaOH2: high pH and hard-tissue response. [Certain]

Limitation: low mechanical strength. [Certain]

RMGIC: stronger, adhesive and fluoride-releasing. [Certain]

Modern principle: selective, not routine, lining. [Certain]

Use the minimum material required for the specific indication. [Likely]

Ensure an effective definitive restoration and coronal seal. [Certain]

## 4. Temporary restorations

Mark allocation: 25 marks.

### Study Mode

#### Definition

A temporary restoration is a restoration intended to protect a tooth and maintain function for a limited period until definitive treatment is completed. [Certain]

#### Objectives

Seal the cavity against oral fluids and bacterial contamination. [Certain]

Protect the pulp from thermal, chemical and mechanical irritation. [Certain]

Maintain tooth position and occlusal relationships where required. [Certain]

Provide adequate function during the interim period. [Certain]

Facilitate endodontic treatment when access to the canal system is required. [Certain]

#### Ideal requirements

Good marginal adaptation and resistance to leakage. [Certain]

Adequate compressive strength for the intended duration. [Certain]

Low solubility in oral fluids. [Certain]

Ease of placement and removal. [Certain]

Compatibility with the definitive restorative material. [Certain]

Biocompatibility. [Certain]

Appropriate radiopacity. [Certain]

#### Common materials

| Material | Advantages | Limitations / cautions |
|---|---|---|
| ZOE | Sealing ability, sedative reputation, easy handling. [Certain] | Eugenol can interfere with resin polymerization. [Certain] |
| IRM | Reinforced ZOE with improved strength for temporary restoration. [Certain] | Still contains eugenol. [Certain] |
| Cavit-type materials | Easy handling and useful for short-term sealing. [Certain] | Relatively low mechanical durability. [Likely] |
| GIC/RMGIC | Adhesion, fluoride release and better durability in selected situations. [Certain] | Moisture control and removal considerations. [Certain] |
| Temporary resin | Useful when greater strength or a provisional crown-like form is required. [Certain] | Polymerization heat can be clinically relevant. [Certain] |

#### Temporary restorations in endodontics

During endodontic treatment, the temporary restoration must prevent coronal leakage between appointments and until the definitive restoration is placed. [Certain] Ingle emphasizes the importance of the coronal restoration and prevention of coronal leakage after root canal treatment. [Certain]

#### Critical considerations

The best temporary material depends on duration, cavity size, occlusal load and whether resin bonding will follow. [Likely]

For short-term endodontic sealing, materials with good adaptation and low solubility are important. [Certain]

When a resin adhesive procedure is planned, noneugenol materials can avoid the polymerization-inhibition problem associated with residual eugenol. [Certain]

Autopolymerizing provisional resins can generate heat during polymerization, so cooling and controlled technique are important when used near vital pulp tissue. [Certain]

### Exam Mode

Definition. [Certain]

Objectives: seal, protect pulp, maintain function and facilitate definitive treatment. [Certain]

Ideal requirements: sealing, strength, low solubility, biocompatibility, radiopacity and easy manipulation/removal. [Certain]

Classify common materials: ZOE, IRM, Cavit-type materials, GIC/RMGIC and temporary resins. [Certain]

ZOE: good seal and long-established temporary use. [Certain]

IRM: reinforced ZOE with improved strength. [Certain]

Cavit-type materials: convenient but mainly suited to limited-duration use. [Likely]

GIC/RMGIC: useful when adhesion and fluoride release are advantageous. [Certain]

Temporary resin: stronger provisional option but polymerization heat must be considered. [Certain]

Endodontic principle: the temporary restoration must maintain a reliable coronal seal until definitive restoration. [Certain]

## 5. Pulp capping materials

Mark allocation: 50 marks.

### Study Mode

#### Definition

Pulp capping is a vital pulp therapy procedure in which a protective material is placed over or near the pulp to preserve vitality and promote repair. [Certain] Direct pulp capping places the material directly over an exposed vital pulp, whereas indirect pulp treatment leaves affected dentin over the pulp and seals it beneath a restoration. [Certain]

#### Objectives

Preserve pulp vitality. [Certain]

Control bacterial contamination. [Certain]

Promote reparative or tertiary dentin formation. [Certain]

Provide a biologically compatible interface between pulp and restoration. [Certain]

Permit long-term maintenance of the tooth when case selection is appropriate. [Certain]

#### Ideal requirements

Biocompatibility. [Certain]

Antibacterial or antimicrobial potential. [Certain]

Ability to promote hard-tissue formation. [Certain]

Dimensional stability and sealing ability. [Certain]

Adequate mechanical integrity. [Certain]

Low solubility. [Certain]

Good handling and radiopacity. [Certain]

#### Calcium hydroxide

Calcium hydroxide has been a traditional pulp-capping material because of its high alkalinity, antibacterial activity and ability to stimulate a hard-tissue response. [Certain] Its disadvantages include low mechanical strength, dissolution over time and formation of a dentin bridge that may contain tunnel defects. [Certain]

#### Mineral trioxide aggregate

MTA is a hydraulic calcium-silicate cement containing major calcium-silicate phases and radiopacifying components. [Certain] It provides favorable sealing and biological properties and has become a major alternative to calcium hydroxide in vital pulp therapy. [Certain] Traditional MTA has been criticized for handling, setting time and discoloration associated with some formulations. [Certain]

#### Biodentine and other hydraulic calcium-silicate cements

Biodentine is a tricalcium-silicate-based material developed as a dentin-replacement and bioactive material. [Certain] Uploaded endodontic sources describe effects on pulp stem-cell proliferation, migration and adhesion and report comparable pulp-capping efficacy with MTA in cited studies. [Certain]

#### Light-cured calcium-silicate materials

Resin-containing light-cured calcium-silicate materials were developed to improve handling and command setting. [Certain] However, systematic-review evidence has indicated limited long-term clinical evidence for direct pulp capping, so they should not automatically be considered equivalent to well-established hydraulic calcium-silicate materials. [Certain]

#### Other materials

ZOE has historically been used in pulp protection, but human evidence has raised concerns about chronic inflammation and inadequate dentin-bridge formation for direct pulp capping. [Certain]

Conventional GIC and RMGIC may be useful as restorative or protective materials in selected settings but are not equivalent to dedicated pulp-capping calcium-silicate materials for direct pulp exposure. [Likely]

Dentin bonding agents have been investigated for pulp protection, but routine direct pulp capping with adhesive systems is not a standard evidence-based replacement for calcium-silicate materials. [Certain]

#### Clinical factors affecting success

Preoperative pulpal diagnosis and case selection. [Certain]

Control of bacterial contamination. [Certain]

Quality of hemostasis where direct pulp exposure is present. [Likely]

Size and nature of exposure. [Likely]

Age and reparative capacity of the pulp. [Likely]

Ability to achieve an immediate and durable coronal seal. [Certain]

#### Current evidence

A 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized clinical trials found that MTA had higher direct-pulp-capping success than calcium hydroxide, while no significant difference was detected between MTA and Biodentine in the included studies; the certainty of evidence was low. [Certain] A separate meta-analysis reported that pooled success rates were generally higher for MTA and Biodentine than for calcium hydroxide over longer follow-up periods, although methodological limitations affected confidence in the estimates. [Certain]

#### Critical appraisal

The weakest part of the traditional pulp-capping model is the assumption that material selection alone determines success. [Certain] Contemporary evidence indicates that diagnosis, inflammation status, contamination control, exposure management and the quality of the final seal are at least as important as the identity of the capping material. [Likely] Thus, a biologically active material cannot compensate for poor case selection or inadequate sealing. [Likely]

#### Comparison

| Material | Main advantages | Main limitations | Evidence position |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ca(OH)2 | High pH, antibacterial action, traditional evidence base. [Certain] | Weak, soluble, tunnel defects. [Certain] | Effective but generally inferior to MTA in comparative clinical success. [Certain] |
| MTA | Biocompatibility, hydraulic setting, sealing and hard-tissue response. [Certain] | Handling, setting time, discoloration in some formulations. [Certain] | Well-established bioactive material; superior to CaOH2 in several analyses. [Certain] |
| Biodentine | Handling, hydraulic calcium-silicate chemistry, bioactivity. [Certain] | Long-term comparative evidence remains less extensive than for MTA. [Likely] | Comparable to MTA in several clinical comparisons. [Certain] |
| Light-cured Ca-silicate | Command set and convenient handling. [Certain] | Resin component and limited long-term DPC evidence. [Certain] | Promising but less established for long-term direct pulp capping. [Certain] |

#### Future directions

Improved hydraulic calcium-silicate formulations with shorter setting time and better handling. [Likely]

Materials engineered to combine bioactivity with predictable adhesion and dimensional stability. [Likely]

Greater emphasis on biologically guided vital pulp therapy rather than material-centred treatment alone. [Likely]

Clinical trials with longer follow-up and standardized diagnostic criteria are needed to distinguish genuine material effects from case-selection effects. [Certain]

## 6. Cements for temporary restorations

Mark allocation: 10 marks.

### Study Mode

#### Definition

Cements for temporary restorations are materials used to seal and protect prepared teeth for a limited period or to temporarily retain provisional restorations. [Certain]

#### Classification

Zinc oxide-eugenol cements. [Certain]

Reinforced zinc oxide-eugenol cements such as IRM. [Certain]

Noneugenol temporary cements. [Certain]

Other temporary luting materials including selected resin or water-based systems according to the clinical situation. [Likely]

#### Zinc oxide-eugenol

ZOE sets by formation of a zinc eugenolate matrix and has long been used because of its sealing characteristics and clinical handling. [Certain] Conventional ZOE has relatively low strength, making it appropriate for temporary rather than definitive high-load applications. [Certain]

#### Reinforced ZOE

IRM is a reinforced ZOE material containing polymer reinforcement and is used where greater strength is desired than conventional ZOE provides. [Certain]

#### Noneugenol materials

Noneugenol temporary cements are useful when the definitive restoration will rely on resin polymerization or adhesive bonding because eugenol can inhibit methacrylate resin polymerization. [Certain]

#### Clinical selection

Short-term temporary restoration with no immediate resin bonding concern: ZOE may be suitable. [Likely]

Higher temporary strength: reinforced ZOE such as IRM may be preferred. [Likely]

Planned adhesive resin procedure: choose a noneugenol alternative where appropriate. [Certain]

Endodontic temporary restoration: prioritize marginal seal, retention and resistance to oral contamination. [Certain]

### Exam Mode

Definition. [Certain]

Classification: ZOE, reinforced ZOE, noneugenol and other temporary luting materials. [Certain]

ZOE composition and setting: zinc oxide plus eugenol producing zinc eugenolate. [Certain]

Advantages: sealing, easy manipulation and established temporary use. [Certain]

Limitations: relatively low strength and eugenol-resin incompatibility. [Certain]

IRM: reinforced ZOE with improved strength. [Certain]

Noneugenol: preferred when subsequent resin bonding/polymerization is anticipated. [Certain]

Clinical selection depends on duration, load, retention and definitive restorative material. [Likely]

Endodontic priority: maintain a reliable coronal seal. [Certain]

Conclusion: choose the temporary cement according to the planned definitive treatment. [Likely]
